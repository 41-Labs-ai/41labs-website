Best AI Automation Agencies in 2026: An Honest Global Shortlist

Nine real agencies compared from what their own websites publish. Pricing where it exists, a best-for verdict per firm, one honest limitation each, including ours, and the vetting questions that separate builders from slide decks.

By Alexander Lee, Founder, 41 Labs

The short answer

There is no single best AI automation agency. There are best picks per job. Axe Automation for systemizing messy operations on no-code tools. Morningside AI if you do not yet know which AI project to do first. AY Automate for an engineer embedded inside your team. Team400 for Australian enterprises that want a local crew in Sydney or Melbourne. 41 Labs, which is us, for small and mid-sized businesses in Singapore or Australia that want fixed project pricing and a free working prototype before paying anything. LeewayHertz for enterprise AI programs, Markovate for industry-specific builds in construction and healthcare, Uvik if you would rather hire senior AI engineers by the hour at a published US$50 to US$99. Every claim on this page comes from what each agency publishes on its own site as of July 2026. Every entry carries one honest limitation, including ours.

How we picked, and why you should distrust every list like this

Every fact below comes from the agency's own website, read in July 2026. Where a firm publishes no pricing, we say so instead of inventing a range. Nobody paid to be on this page and we run no affiliate deals.

Two disclosures before the list. First, 41 Labs, at number five, is our company. We gave ourselves one slot, held it to the same format as everyone else, and put our limitation in the same place theirs sits. Second, ranking lists written by agencies are marketing. Ours included. Team400 publishes its own top-agencies list with itself in it, and so do half the firms that show up for this search. So skip the order, read the limitations, and shortlist the two or three firms whose delivery model matches how you want to work.

Done-for-you automation agencies

These firms own the outcome. You describe the problem, they map the process, build the system, and hand it over working.

1. Axe Automation: best for systemizing operations on no-code stacks

Axe Automation audits how your business runs, rebuilds the operations on platforms like Monday.com, then installs automations with Make, Zapier, and n8n. The site claims 400+ clients, 1,000+ workflows automated, and 100,000 manual hours saved, with names like ESPN and JLL on the client wall. Make.com named it Partner of the Year for North America in 2025. If your company runs on spreadsheets and copy-paste, and you want a team that fixes the process before automating it, this is the strongest US pick.

Axe publishes no pricing, so you walk into the sales call blind. The work also rides on no-code platforms, which means the Monday.com, Make, and Zapier subscriptions keep billing long after the agency leaves. And the homepage calls it the number one AI automation agency, which is a self-award. Every superlative on this page, including any of ours, deserves the same discount.

2. Morningside AI: best when you do not know what to automate yet

Morningside AI runs a three-step engagement: find the highest-impact opportunity, build it, then train your team to use it. The site lists 48+ client engagements across 11+ industries, with the Milwaukee Bucks, BarkBox, and the Sydney Roosters among them, and describes the team as workflow specialists first, AI specialists second. That framing earns the slot. Most automation projects die at project selection, long before any code gets written, so an agency that starts with the workflow audit is starting in the right place.

Morningside publishes no pricing. It also runs a large education arm, with a YouTube audience the site puts at 13.4 million views and a 90,000-person community. Nothing wrong with that, but ask early who will work on your account: the people you watched, or a delivery team behind them.

3. AY Automate: best for an engineer embedded inside your team

AY Automate places what it calls a forward deployed engineer inside your company. The engineer learns how the work happens day to day, then builds AI systems that take the repetitive parts off your plate. The site lists 45+ companies served across 15+ industries, from law firms to logistics, and backs the model with a guarantee that month one is fully refundable.

The public track record is thinner than the bigger names here. There is no published pricing, and the site names no headquarters. The refund guarantee exists to offset exactly that risk, and it does help. Get the refund terms in writing before you start.

Regional specialists

Two firms whose value is depth in one market instead of thin coverage everywhere.

4. Team400: best for Australian enterprises that want a local team

Team400 is an Australian engineering firm with teams in Sydney, Melbourne, and Brisbane. It positions itself bluntly as engineers who build and deploy production systems rather than advisors who present findings, claims 50+ AI agents deployed for Australian businesses, and names enterprise clients including Telstra, Kmart, and Medibank. If you want people in the same city and references at that scale, this is the local pick.

Enterprise references cut both ways. What works for Telstra says little about a 12-person business with a A$20,000 budget, and Team400 publishes no project pricing. It also writes its own top-agencies lists with itself ranked first, which is exactly the genre you are reading now, so weigh its list and this one with the same suspicion.

5. 41 Labs: best for SMEs in Singapore and Australia that want proof before payment

41 Labs is our company, so read this entry knowing who wrote it. We are a Singapore AI agency building custom systems trained on each client's own data: quoting automation, document handling, back-office AI, and WhatsApp sales agents through our managed product, 41 Closer. We have delivered 50+ systems and every one of them is still running in a real business today. The case we cite most is a quoting workflow we cut from 3 hours to 5 minutes per quote, with errors down 90% and the same team handling 35% more volume.

Projects run S$7,000 to S$25,000, roughly A$8,000 to A$29,000, and before you pay anything we build a free working prototype on your real data, usually inside 2 weeks, with full deployment in 3 to 8 weeks. What we publish for each market is on our Singapore and Australia pages. Now the limitations, listed in the same spot as everyone else's. We have no US delivery track record yet. Our case studies are in Singapore and our Australian delivery is fully remote, so there is no office to visit and nobody to meet for coffee. And our agents are text based, not voice. If your leads phone in instead of messaging, we are not the fit, and we will say so on the first call.

Enterprise AI consultancies

When the project spans departments, compliance teams, and six-figure budgets, the boutiques above stop being the right shape.

6. LeewayHertz: best for enterprise AI programs

LeewayHertz has built AI and software for over 15 years, for clients its site describes as running from startups to the Fortune 500, and it developed ZBrain, its own enterprise AI platform. Forbes has listed it among top AI consulting firms, Gartner named it a representative vendor in the 2024 Hype Cycle for Generative AI, and The Hackett Group acquired it. That resume tells you the scale it operates at.

The scale is also the limitation. If your budget tops out at five figures, you are not the customer this machinery was built for, and the process overhead that reassures a Fortune 500 procurement team will slow a 30-person company to a crawl.

7. Markovate: best for industry-specific AI in construction, healthcare, and manufacturing

Markovate is a US generative AI development company that concentrates on five industries: manufacturing, construction, healthcare, insurance, and real estate. The specialization shows in what it sells. CAD-to-BOM extraction, construction takeoff estimation, and medical coding automation instead of generic chatbot builds. The site claims 50+ AI projects delivered, holds ISO 9001 and ISO 27001 certifications, and supports on-premise and air-gapped deployments for companies that cannot send data to the cloud.

No pricing is published, and the five-industry focus is a real boundary. Outside those verticals you are buying general capability, which is fine, but it is not the specialization that earned Markovate this slot.

Different models worth knowing

Two firms that are not done-for-you agencies at all, included because for some buyers their shape is the better answer.

8. Uvik: best for hiring senior AI engineers by the hour

Uvik is a staff augmentation firm from Tallinn, Estonia that embeds senior Python and AI engineers into your team at a published US$50 to US$99 per hour, with a claimed seniority floor of 7 years and no juniors. It is one of the only firms on this page that puts real prices on its website, and it holds a 5.0 rating on Clutch across 30+ reviews. If you have technical leadership and simply need more hands, hourly senior engineers beat agency margins.

The limitation is built into the model. You manage the engineers and you own the outcome, so if nobody in your business can write a spec or review technical work, an hourly engineer will sit waiting for direction you cannot give. A done-for-you agency higher up this list will serve you better.

9. Goodish Agency: best for marketing-side automation

Goodish Agency, based in Dubai, runs AI and automation inside a marketing practice: CRM and workflow automation, paid advertising, analytics, and web development, as a Google, Meta, and Zoho partner serving 40+ businesses. If the automation you need lives in your marketing stack, lead routing, CRM hygiene, campaign reporting, this shape of firm fits better than a pure engineering shop.

The flip side is that operations automation is not the center of gravity here. Quoting engines, document processing, and back-office AI belong with the firms earlier in this list, and Goodish publishes no pricing. More on AI quote automation.

What does an AI automation agency actually do?

Strip the label off and the job has four parts. The agency maps one of your processes end to end, finds the repetitive 80% of it, builds software that does that 80%, and leaves the judgment calls with your people. The build itself might be no-code integrations, custom code, or an AI agent that reads documents and writes replies. Whether any of it pays off depends less on the model and more on whether the agency understood your process. We wrote a separate piece on whether AI automation actually works, with the failure cases included.

The four delivery models on this list handle that job differently. Done-for-you agencies own the outcome. Embedded-engineer firms rent you a builder, staff augmentation rents you capacity, and the enterprise consultancies sell you a program. None of these is wrong. They fail when a buyer picks the wrong one for their situation, like a two-person team buying enterprise consulting, or a non-technical founder hiring hourly engineers and hoping direction will sort itself out.

What should you pay?

Published numbers put the market in bands, and the gaps between bands are wide. Off-the-shelf tools like Zapier and Make run about S$50 to S$500 per month if you build with them yourself. Staff augmentation is hourly. Uvik publishes US$50 to US$99. Specialist agencies charge per project, and the S$7,000 to S$25,000 we publish is typical of the boutique band. Large consultancies start around S$50,000 and run to S$500,000 or more for multi-phase enterprise programs.

The sticker price is only half the bill. No-code builds keep paying platform subscriptions after handover. AI systems keep paying model usage fees per document or per conversation. An agency that quotes you a build fee without the ongoing number is quoting half the cost, so ask for the all-in monthly figure a year after launch, in writing.

How to vet an AI automation agency

Most agencies on this list publish no pricing and every one of them claims a track record. The only way through is to ask questions that are expensive to fake. These seven have served us and our clients well, and we would answer all of them ourselves on a first call.

  • "Show me one system live in production right now, and let me talk to that client." Screenshots and demo videos are rehearsed. A client on the phone is not.
  • "Who owns the code and the accounts when we part ways?" If the workflows live in the agency's Make account, leaving costs you the system.
  • "What is the all-in monthly cost a year after launch?" Platform subscriptions plus AI usage fees plus support. Half quotes hide this.
  • "Can I see something working before I pay?" A prototype, a pilot, a live demo agent. Any form works. We do a free prototype on your real data, and we hold competitors to the bar we set ourselves.
  • "Who exactly does the work?" The person selling and the person building are usually different people. Meet the builder.
  • "What happens when it breaks at 9pm?" Every automation breaks eventually. The answer tells you if they have run systems in production or just launched them.
  • "Tell me about a project that failed." Anyone who has shipped 50 projects has a failure story. An agency with none is either new or lying.

Red flags, from the buying mistakes we hear about most. Pay-per-result billing on automation work sounds safe and almost never survives contact with reality, because nobody can define the result precisely enough to bill on it, and the arrangement collapses into disputes. No production references means you are the reference. A firm that promises to automate your business before seeing your process is selling capability, not outcomes. And pressure to sign a 12-month retainer before a single project has shipped moves all the risk to you.

The single most useful number in this market is how few projects ship at all. On our own agency pages we cite the industry estimate that 80% of AI projects never make it into production. That is the stat to vet against. It is why "show me a running system" beats every other question on the list.

Agency or DIY tools?

Plenty of automation needs no agency at all. Zapier, Make, and n8n plus a motivated staff member will connect your forms to your CRM and your CRM to your invoicing for S$50 to S$500 a month, and a weekend of tinkering. If you want to see what building an agent yourself involves before paying anyone, we wrote a walkthrough on how to create an AI agent.

The line where DIY stops making sense is measured in money, not ambition. When the process touches revenue, when an error costs a customer or a compliance problem, or when the honest answer to "who maintains this?" is nobody, the S$50 tool becomes the expensive option. A quoting process that burns 3 hours a quote across 20 quotes a week is 60 staff hours. Against that, a S$15,000 project pays for itself in months, which is the arithmetic behind every good agency engagement. Run that math on your own process before you talk to anyone on this list, including us.

Frequently asked questions

How much does an AI automation agency cost?

Published numbers put the market in three bands. Off-the-shelf automation tools run about S$50 to S$500 per month if you set them up yourself. Specialist agencies charge S$7,000 to S$25,000 per project, the band 41 Labs works in. Large consultancies charge S$50,000 to S$500,000 or more. Staff augmentation sits in between. Uvik publishes US$50 to US$99 per hour for senior engineers. Most agencies on this list publish no pricing at all, so ask for the all-in number, including tool subscriptions and AI usage fees, before you sign anything.

Are AI automation agencies worth it?

They are worth it when the process you want to automate costs more per year than the project fee, and when nobody on your team can own the build. A quoting process that eats 3 hours per quote justifies a S$15,000 project quickly. They are not worth it for a task you run twice a month, or when a S$50 per month Zapier plan and one motivated staff member can do the job. Price is rarely the real problem anyway. Delivery is. Roughly 80% of AI projects never make it into production, so judge any agency on running systems rather than proposals.

What is the best AI automation agency for small business?

For most small businesses the answer is whichever agency gives you a fixed price and proof before payment. Axe Automation and Morningside AI are strong US picks for ops systemization and first-project scoping. In Singapore and Australia, 41 Labs, our own firm, builds a free working prototype on your real data before you pay, with projects at S$7,000 to S$25,000. If your budget is under about S$5,000, skip agencies for now and start with DIY tools like Zapier or Make plus a few hours of a freelancer's time.

How do I know if an AI agency is legitimate?

Ask for one system that is live in production today and a client you can call about it. Slide decks, mockups, and follower counts prove nothing. Ask who owns the code when you leave, what the all-in monthly cost is including tool and AI usage fees, and what happens after launch. Walk away from pay-per-result billing on automation work, since nobody can define the result, and from anyone who promises to automate your business before seeing your process. A legitimate agency will also tell you when you should not buy.

What are the best AI automation agencies?

There is no global winner. The category splits by what you actually need. For productised workflow automation at speed, Axe Automation and AY Automate are the names that come up most in the US market. For enterprise programmes with governance attached, LeewayHertz and Markovate operate at that scale. In Australia, Team400. In Singapore, the market is smaller and mostly boutique, us included. The useful filter is not the ranking, it is the shape of the work: agencies that build and hand over suit companies with an internal team to inherit it, and agencies that build and operate suit companies without one. Most disappointment comes from buying the first kind while needing the second.

I have heard good things about AI automation agencies. Which ones are actually trustworthy?

Ignore the case studies. They are always the wins. Judge on four things instead. Ask for a project they stopped and why, because an agency that has never killed one is either new or editing. Ask who writes the code and whether you can meet them before signing, since the people who pitch are often not the people who build. Ask what happens in month nine when the thing needs changing and the original builder has moved on. And ask what they will not take on, because an agency with no refusals has no method. Two red flags worth naming: pricing that will not commit to a number until they know your budget, and demos running on the vendor's own data rather than yours. Ask to see it work on your catalogue, your prices, your messy real records.

What is the best AI automation agency for enterprises?

For genuine enterprise scale, meaning multi-country rollout, procurement, security review and change management, the realistic shortlist is the large firms: Accenture, Deloitte, or regionally NCS in Singapore. Among specialists, LeewayHertz and Markovate take enterprise engagements. The trade-off is consistent: large firms give you assurance, coverage and someone to hold accountable, and they charge partner rates for a delivery team you did not choose. Specialists move faster and cost less but will not absorb a global change programme. A test that cuts through it: if the hardest part of your project is getting twenty stakeholders to agree, buy the large firm. If the hardest part is the engineering, buy the specialist and keep the stakeholder work in-house.

What are the top automation agencies right now?

By market, not one list. United States: Axe Automation, AY Automate, Morningside AI, with LeewayHertz and Markovate for larger builds. Australia: Team400. Singapore and Southeast Asia: a boutique field including 41 Labs, Voltade and TechTiq. Enterprise anywhere: Accenture, Deloitte, NCS. Rankings in this category move quickly and most published lists, including ones we appear on, are assembled from what each agency writes about itself. Treat any list as a shortlist to interview rather than a verdict, and weight recency, since an agency's team can turn over substantially in a year and the case study you liked may have been built by people who left.
