Wati Alternatives in 2026: 7 Options, Real Pricing, Honest Trade-offs

Published pricing, checked 15 September 2026. What each alternative is actually better at, and the case for staying on Wati.

By Alexander Lee, Founder, 41 Labs

The short answer

The closest like-for-like Wati alternative is Interakt at US$55 a month, which does the same WhatsApp-first job with a lighter CRM. If you need more than WhatsApp, respond.io from US$79 with AI from US$159 is the stronger engine. For Asia-focused omnichannel, SleekFlow has a free plan. For social-first businesses, ManyChat from US$14. For developers, Twilio at about US$0.005 a message. If nobody will operate it, buy a managed service rather than software.

First, the case for staying on Wati

Wati is a good product and most people who shop for an alternative end up staying. It is WhatsApp-first rather than WhatsApp-as-an-afterthought, the shared team inbox is solid, and broadcasts work. The three reasons people genuinely outgrow it: they need channels beyond WhatsApp and end up paying for a second tool, additional users at US$39 to US$89 each start to dominate the bill as the team grows, or they wanted an AI that quotes from a real price list and found the AI co-pilot assists an agent rather than replacing one. If none of those applies, the migration is not worth the fortnight it will cost you.

The alternatives, by the job they do

These tools are not interchangeable and comparing them on price alone will pick the wrong one. The column that matters most is the last one: who is going to operate this thing in month six.

How to choose in five minutes

Answer three questions honestly. Which channel do your customers actually message you on? Not which you wish they used. If it is WhatsApp, buy a WhatsApp-first tool. If it is Instagram, buy a social-first one. Who owns this in month six? Every platform here needs a person to build flows, fix template rejections and keep follow-ups current, and the most common failure is not choosing wrong but choosing well and then nobody maintaining it. What is one enquiry worth? At twenty dollars, buy the cheapest thing that works. At two thousand, the difference between a reply in five minutes and a reply tomorrow morning is worth more than the entire software bill.

The option that is not software

Every tool above is something your team runs. That is the right shape if you have someone to run it. If you do not, the honest alternative is a managed service, where the agent is built, operated and tuned for you and nothing lands on your team's plate. That is what 41 Closer is, from S$690 a month, and we would rather say plainly that it is the wrong purchase if you have a capable ops person who enjoys this work. In that case buy Wati or respond.io and keep the difference.

Worth knowing before you decide either way: we messaged 25 Singapore businesses on WhatsApp in the evening and timed the replies. The median wait for a human answer was 13.3 hours and four never replied at all. The full study is here. Whichever tool you pick, that gap is the thing you are actually buying your way out of.

Frequently asked questions

What is the best alternative to Wati?

There is no single best, only the best for the channel you sell on. For a WhatsApp-first inbox, Wati at US$59 a month or Interakt at US$55. For multi-channel at scale, respond.io from US$79 with AI from US$159. For Asia-focused omnichannel, SleekFlow, which has a free plan. For social-first selling, ManyChat from US$14. For developers, Twilio at about US$0.005 a message. If the honest problem is that nobody on your team will operate any of them, the alternative is a managed service rather than another piece of software. Prices checked 15 September 2026.

How much does Wati cost?

Wati publishes Growth US$59, Pro US$119 and Business US$279 a month billed annually, with extra users at US$39 to US$89 each. Meta's own per-conversation WhatsApp fee is charged on top, and it is easy to underestimate at volume. Checked 15 September 2026. Two things to price properly before comparing: whether the AI features you want sit on the plan you are quoting or an upgrade above it, and what additional users cost, because on several platforms in this category the seat price rather than the base plan is what grows the bill.

Is it worth switching from Wati?

Usually not, unless one specific thing is broken. Migration costs a fortnight of somebody's attention: rebuilding flows, re-approving message templates with Meta, retraining the team, and re-testing everything. That is worth paying if you are on the wrong channel, if seat costs have outgrown the value, or if you need an AI that quotes from a real price list rather than following a script. It is not worth paying to save twenty dollars a month. Write down the one thing that is actually failing before you shop, and if you cannot name it, stay.

Do I have to change my WhatsApp number if I switch platforms?

No, but it is the step that most often goes wrong. Your WhatsApp Business API number is registered to a Meta Business account, and moving it means migrating between Business Solution Providers rather than getting a new number. It is a supported process and it takes a few days. Two cautions: you cannot use the WhatsApp Business app and the API on the same number at once, and message templates usually have to be resubmitted for approval on the new provider. Plan the switch for a quiet week, not a campaign week.
