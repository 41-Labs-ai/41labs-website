What it does inside your sale

Step by step, in your flow, not a generic chatbot script

  1. It answers the vacancy question. A parent asks if there is a place for P1 in January or for K1 next term. The agent checks the places list you gave us, or on Pro your live sheet, and says yes, no, or waitlist. Then it asks which school or how old the child is.
  2. It quotes the fee and what is included. Five days or three, term time or holiday programme only, meals in or out. It quotes from your fee sheet and says plainly whether a subsidy applies, in your words. It never rounds a fee or guesses one.
  3. It asks the one question that qualifies the lead. Which school, which level next year, and when the child starts. A parent with a real child answers in a line. The form-fillers who only wanted the fee drop out here, which one centre told us was many of its ad leads.
  4. It books the centre visit or the open house slot. It offers two times from your calendar, takes the parent's name and the child's name, and confirms the slot in the chat. If the parent asked the fee and went quiet, it follows up.
  5. It hands over to the principal. A subsidy question beyond your sheet, a child with late dismissal, special needs, a parent who wants to negotiate the fee. The agent says it will have the principal reply, and you get the whole conversation in one message with the name, the child's level and the visit time.

What it connects to. On Starter it reads your fee sheet, your places list by level, the schools you escort from, your hours and open house dates, and your calendar link for visits, all sent to us on WhatsApp. On Pro it reads and writes the Google Sheet you track places and visits in, or the enrolment system you already use, so the vacancy answer is live and every booked visit lands in your sheet. If you charge a registration fee, Pro takes it in the chat with a payment link.

What preschools and student care centres said to us, and our answer

Paraphrased from our calls. No business is named.

"We get four or five enquiries a month outside intake season. An auto-reply on WhatsApp Business is enough for that."

Then do not buy this yet. At four or five a month, a WhatsApp Business auto-reply and a habit of replying the same day will do, and it costs nothing. Buy it when the open house ads or the October to December intake push you past what one person can answer the same day. One centre told us it aimed for 50 to 60 enquiries in its fourth quarter. At that volume one person cannot answer the same day, and that is when it is worth S$690.

"Half the ad leads have odd names and never answer, even when we follow up every one."

The agent cannot fix a bad audience, so if the ads bring junk, fix the ads first. What it changes is the first minute. It asks the one question a real parent answers at once, which level and which school, and the form-fillers who only wanted the fee drop out on their own. Point the ad at WhatsApp rather than a lead form, because a form lead waits for a person to download it, and the agent can only answer a parent who messages.

"Our families come by referral. They want to talk to a person, not a bot."

They still will. A referred parent messages at 9pm asking if there is a place in K1, and what she wants at 9pm is the answer and a visit slot. The agent gives her both, says it is an assistant if she asks, and the principal meets her at the visit. One preschool told us it grew on referrals and still converted about 3 in 100 enquiries. Referred parents message at night too.

"My staff are not technical. They struggled to edit our own website. We cannot run another system."

You do not run anything. On Starter the only system is your own WhatsApp. When a fee changes or the open house date moves, you send it to us in a message and we update the agent. On Pro it reads from the sheet you already keep, so the only habit is keeping that sheet current.
